# androgenic-alopecia-pde3b-1so2-analysis-m.sc-microproject-second-semester

This repository contains the materials related to my M.Sc. Bioinformatics second-semester micro project entitled **"Elucidating Mechanism of Drugs to Treat Androgenic Alopecia: A Case Study of Human Phosphodiesterase 3B (PDB ID: 1SO2)."**
The project investigates the structural and molecular interactions between Human Phosphodiesterase 3B (PDE3B) and its inhibitor using computational biology and molecular visualization approaches.

# Objectives:
* Retrieve and analyze the crystal structure of PDE3B.
* Study protein–ligand interactions.
* Visualize the active site and binding pocket.
* Identify key amino acid residues involved in ligand binding.
* Understand the molecular basis of inhibitor action.

# Protein Information:

# Methodology:

1. Retrieved the PDE3B crystal structure (PDB ID: 1SO2) from the RCSB Protein Data Bank.
2. Performed structural validation and superimposition with PDE4B (PDB ID: 4KP6) using Swiss-PdbViewer.
3. Prepared the protein structure using Schrödinger Maestro.
4. Obtained ligand structures from PubChem and prepared them using LigPrep.
5. Generated the receptor grid and performed molecular docking in Maestro.
6. Analyzed docking scores and protein–ligand interactions.
7. Visualized binding interactions and interpreted the results.

# Results:

The crystal structure of Human Phosphodiesterase 3B (PDE3B) complexed with a dihydropyridazine inhibitor (PDB ID: 1SO2) was successfully analyzed using computational structural biology approaches.

Structural validation and superimposition studies were performed using Swiss-PdbViewer. Superimposition of PDE3B (1SO2) with PDE4B (4KP6) produced an RMSD value of 1.02 Å, indicating a high degree of structural similarity between the two phosphodiesterase family proteins.

Molecular docking studies were conducted using Schrödinger Maestro. Among the screened compounds, Taxifolin demonstrated the strongest binding affinity with a docking score of −11.9 kcal/mol. The reference ligand associated with 1SO2 showed a docking score of −11.3 kcal/mol, while Minoxidil exhibited a docking score of −6.7 kcal/mol.

Protein–ligand interaction analysis identified multiple stabilizing interactions within the active site, including hydrogen-bonding and hydrophobic contacts. These findings suggest that Taxifolin possesses favorable binding characteristics and may serve as a promising candidate for further investigation in androgenic alopecia research.

The study highlights the application of molecular docking and structural bioinformatics techniques for understanding protein–ligand interactions and evaluating potential therapeutic compounds.
